# 069 — Kernbach 2013: Unconventional Research in USSR and Russia

## Metadata
- **Title:** Unconventional research in USSR and Russia: short overview
- **Author:** Serge Kernbach
- **Year:** 2013
- **Journal:** arXiv (preprint)
- **arXiv:** 1312.1148
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1148

## Abstract

A survey of unconventional research in Russia from the end of the 19th until the beginning of the 21st centuries, covering areas related to generation and detection of "high-penetrating" emission of non-biological origin. The paper discusses relations to modern studies on biological effects of weak electromagnetic emission, bioinformatics, and theories of physical vacuum. The overview documents a Soviet research program that reportedly spent $0.5-1 billion, covering topics including instrumental psychotronics (sometimes denoted as "torsionics"), torsion field generators, distant interaction experiments, and related military applications.

## Key Topics Covered
- Soviet psychotronics research programs (1960s-1990s)
- Torsion field generators (Beridze-Stakhovsky's "Cerpan")
- Kaznacheev's distant interaction experiments (see paper 061)
- Kozyrev mirror experiments
- Biological effects of weak electromagnetic emission
- Military applications of psychoacoustic research
- Estimated program budget: $0.5-1 billion

## Scientific Status

**Tier D — Historical overview of unconventional programs.** This is not a peer-reviewed journal article but an arXiv preprint that documents the scope and institutional investment of Soviet unconventional research. The value is historical context, not scientific validation. Many of the individual experiments described remain unreplicated by Western laboratories.

## Relevance to Aureonoise

Historical context only.
