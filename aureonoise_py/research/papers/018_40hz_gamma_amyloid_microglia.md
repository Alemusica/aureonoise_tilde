# 018 — 40 Hz Gamma: Amyloid and Microglia (MIT/Tsai Lab)

## Metadata
- **Title:** Gamma frequency entrainment attenuates amyloid load and modifies microglia
- **Authors:** Hunter F Iaccarino, Annabelle C Singer, Anthony J Martorell, Andrii Rudenko, Fan Gao, Tyler Z Gillingham, Hansruedi Mathys, Jinsoo Seo, Oleg Kritskiy, Fatema Abdurrob, Chinnakkaruppan Adaikkan, Rebecca G Canter, Richard Rueda, Emery N Brown, Edward S Boyden, Li-Huei Tsai
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Nature
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature20587
- **PMC:** PMC5656389
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5656389/

## Abstract
Changes in gamma oscillations (20-50 Hz) have been observed in several neurological disorders. However, the relationship between gamma and cellular pathologies is unclear. Here, we show reduced behaviorally-driven gamma before the onset of plaque formation or cognitive decline in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Optogenetically driving FS-PV-interneurons at gamma (40 Hz), but not other frequencies, reduced levels of amyloid-beta 1-40 and 1-42 isoforms. Gene expression profiling revealed induction of genes associated with morphological transformation of microglia and histological analysis confirmed increased microglia co-localization with amyloid-beta. Subsequently, we designed a non-invasive 40 Hz light-flickering paradigm that reduced amyloid-beta levels in visual cortex of pre-depositing mice and mitigated plaque load in aged, depositing mice.

## Key Findings
- 40 Hz optogenetic stimulation reduced amyloid-beta 1-40 by 53% and 1-42 by 45%
- Non-invasive 40 Hz light flicker reduced visual cortex amyloid by ~58%
- 7-day treatment reduced plaque number by 67% and size by 64%
- Gamma stimulation triggered microglia morphological changes for enhanced phagocytosis
- Effect was specific to 40 Hz — other frequencies did not produce the same effect
