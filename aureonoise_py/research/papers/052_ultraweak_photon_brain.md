# 052 — Ultraweak Photon Emission in the Brain

## Metadata
- **Title:** Ultraweak photon emission in the brain
- **Authors:** V Salari, H Valian, H Bassereh, I Bokkon, A Barkhordari
- **Year:** 2015
- **Journal:** Journal of Integrative Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.1142/S0219635215300012
- **PubMed:** 26336891
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26336891/

## Abstract
Besides the low-frequency electromagnetic body-processes measurable through the electroencephalography (EEG), electrocardiography (ECG), etc. there are processes that do not need external excitation, emitting light within or close to the visible spectra. Such ultraweak photon emission (UPE), also named biophoton emission, reflects the cellular (and body) oxidative status. Recently, a growing body of evidence shows that UPE may play an important role in the basic functioning of living cells. Moreover, interesting evidences are beginning to emerge that UPE may well play an important role in neuronal functions. In fact, biophotons are byproducts in cellular metabolism and produce false signals (e.g., retinal discrete dark noise) but on the other side neurons contain many light sensitive molecules that makes it hard to imagine how they might not be influenced by UPE, and thus UPE may carry informational contents.

## Key Findings
- UPE reflects cellular oxidative status and may carry informational content
- Neurons contain many light-sensitive molecules
- Biophotons produce retinal dark noise (false visual signals)
- UPE may play a functional role in neuronal communication
- Theoretical models suggest photons could mediate inter-neuronal signaling

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The presence of light-sensitive molecules in neurons means the brain has endogenous optical detection capability. While Aureonoise works through auditory pathways, this research suggests the brain uses multiple wave-based communication channels. The connection to oxidative status links to the metabolic health aspect of Aureonoise's therapeutic goals — coherent brain states may reduce oxidative stress.
