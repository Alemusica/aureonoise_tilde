# 003 — Alpha-Frequency Stimulation and DMN Connectivity

## Metadata
- **Title:** Alpha-Frequency Stimulation Enhances Synchronization of Alpha Oscillations with Default Mode Network Connectivity
- **Authors:** Yijia Ma, Joshua A Brown, Chaowen Chen, Mingzhou Ding, Wei Wu, Wen Li
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** eNeuro
- **DOI:** 10.1523/ENEURO.0449-24.2025
- **URL:** https://www.eneuro.org/content/12/3/ENEURO.0449-24.2025

## Abstract
Alpha (8-12 Hz) oscillations and default mode network (DMN) activity dominate the brain's intrinsic activity in the temporal and spatial domains, respectively. They are thought to play crucial roles in the spatiotemporal organization of the complex brain system. Relatedly, both have been implicated, often concurrently, in diverse neuropsychiatric disorders, with accruing electroencephalogram (EEG)/magnetoencephalogram and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data linking these two neural activities both at rest and during key cognitive operations. Prominent theories and extant findings thus converge to suggest a mechanistic relationship between alpha oscillations and the DMN. Here, we leveraged simultaneous EEG-fMRI data acquired before and after alpha-frequency transcranial alternating current stimulation (alpha-tACS) and observed that alpha-tACS tightened the dynamic coupling between spontaneous fluctuations in alpha power and DMN connectivity (especially, in the posterior DMN, between the posterior cingulate cortex and the bilateral angular gyrus). In comparison, no significant changes were observed for temporal correlations between power in other oscillatory frequencies and connectivity in other major networks. These results thus suggest an inherent coupling between alpha and DMN activity in humans. Importantly, these findings highlight the efficacy of alpha-tACS in regulating the DMN, a clinically significant network that is challenging to target directly with noninvasive methods.

## Key Findings
- alpha-tACS significantly increased coupling between alpha power and posterior DMN connectivity
- Effects were specific to alpha frequency; no changes for theta or beta
- Posterior DMN (PCC to bilateral angular gyrus) is the primary target
- Alpha stimulation is an effective noninvasive method to regulate DMN

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Direct evidence that external alpha-frequency stimulation modulates DMN connectivity. This validates Aureonoise's core mechanism: auditory alpha-frequency stimulation can regulate the same network. The specificity to posterior DMN (PCC-angular gyrus) informs spatial targeting via bilateral audio presentation.
