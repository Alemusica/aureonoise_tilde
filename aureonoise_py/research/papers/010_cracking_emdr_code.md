# 010 — Cracking the EMDR Code

## Metadata
- **Title:** Cracking the EMDR code: Recruitment of sensory, memory and emotional networks during bilateral alternating auditory stimulation
- **Authors:** Pierre-Francois Rousseau, Sarah Boukezzi, Rene Garcia, Thierry Chaminade, Stephanie Khalfa
- **Year:** 2020
- **Journal:** Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry
- **DOI:** 10.1177/0004867420913623
- **PubMed:** 32271126
- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0004867420913623

## Abstract
The researchers demonstrated that bilateral alternating stimulation is not a simple sensory signal and can activate large emotional neural networks. 38 healthy participants underwent functional MRI scanning across two consecutive days, performing fear conditioning and extinction procedures with and without bilateral alternating stimulation.

## Key Findings
- Bilateral alternating stimulation activates auditory areas, right precuneus, and left medial frontal gyrus
- Enhanced connectivity among temporal regions, precuneus, and emotional processing structures
- BLS is not a simple sensory signal — it recruits large-scale emotional and memory networks
- The precuneus activation links BLS to DMN/self-referential processing

## Relevance to Aureonoise
This is the most directly relevant paper for Aureonoise's bilateral mechanism. Bilateral alternating auditory stimulation activates the precuneus (a core DMN hub) and engages emotional/memory networks. This validates Aureonoise's alternating bilateral audio as a genuine neuromodulation tool, not just a sensory distraction. The precuneus activation connects bilateral stimulation to the DMN pathway established in papers 001-006.
