# 051 — Biophoton and Brain Activity (PNAS 2016)

## Metadata
- **Title:** Human high intelligence is involved in spectral redshift of biophotonic activities in the brain
- **Authors:** Zhuo Wang, Niting Wang, Zhihui Li, Fangyan Xiao, Jiapei Dai
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.1604855113
- **PubMed:** 27432962
- **URL:** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1604855113

## Abstract
The research examines how neural communication and information processing in circuits support various cognitive functions. The authors propose that enhanced cognitive ability depends on more efficient neural signaling requiring less energy. The paper discusses how spectral redshift of biophotonic activities may relate to intelligence levels in the human brain.

## Key Findings
- Biophotonic activity in brain tissue shows spectral redshift correlated with intelligence
- More efficient neural signaling requires less energy and produces different photon spectra
- Neural biophotons may carry functional information, not just metabolic byproduct
- The spectral characteristics of brain biophotons differ from other tissues
