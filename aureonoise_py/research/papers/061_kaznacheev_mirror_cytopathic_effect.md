# 061 — Kaznacheev 1980: Distant Intercellular Electromagnetic Interaction

## Metadata
- **Title:** Distant intercellular electromagnetic interaction between two tissue cultures
- **Authors:** VP Kaznacheev, LP Mikhailova, NB Kartashov
- **Year:** 1980
- **Journal:** Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine (Byulleten' Eksperimental'noi Biologii i Meditsiny)
- **Volume/Pages:** 89:345-348
- **DOI:** 10.1007/BF00834249
- **PubMed:** 6248145
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00834249

## Abstract

Experimental study of distant intercellular electromagnetic interaction between two tissue cultures. Two sealed tissue cultures (animal or human cells) are placed on separate plates and connected via either a quartz window (UV-transparent) or a glass window (UV-opaque). One culture (inducer) is infected/killed with a virus or chemical toxin (mercuric chloride). Through quartz: the healthy culture (detector) develops the same cytopathic response — dying in the same pattern as the inducer, without chemical contact. Through glass: no effect on the detector culture.

The communicating signal is in the ultraviolet range (200-400 nm), as quartz transmits UV while glass blocks UV below ~300 nm. The effect showed seasonal variation (strongest in August).

## Key Findings
- Cells communicate at distance through electromagnetic radiation in the UV range
- The "mirror cytopathic effect": healthy cells mirror the death pattern of infected cells through UV-transparent barriers
- No chemical contact required — purely electromagnetic communication
- UV biophoton emission from dying cells carries enough information to induce the same pathology
- The effect is blocked by UV-opaque barriers (glass) — confirming UV as the carrier
- Signal intensity: extremely weak (10^-17 to 10^-23 W/cm^2)

## Scientific Status

**Tier C — Published but not independently replicated.** The paper appeared in a respected Soviet journal (Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine, published by Springer in English translation). However, no independent Western replication has been published. The concept of biophoton emission from cells is now mainstream (see papers 050-052), but the specific mirror cytopathic effect remains unconfirmed.
