# 067 — Shapiro 1989: Original EMDR Paper

## Metadata
- **Title:** Efficacy of the eye movement desensitization procedure in the treatment of traumatic memories
- **Author:** Francine Shapiro
- **Year:** 1989
- **Journal:** Journal of Traumatic Stress
- **Volume/Pages:** 2(2):199-223
- **DOI:** 10.1002/jts.2490020207
- **URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jts.2490020207

## Abstract

The first controlled study of Eye Movement Desensitization (EMD), the precursor to EMDR. Shapiro treated 22 patients with persisting traumatic memories of childhood sexual abuse, physical and sexual assaults, emotional abuse, and experiences from the Vietnam War. Using a single session of EMD (saccadic eye movements while attending to the traumatic memory), she found dramatic improvements in subjective distress levels (SUD scores), with results maintained at 3-month follow-up. The study used a delayed treatment control design with 22 subjects randomized to immediate treatment or delayed treatment groups.

## Key Findings
- Single-session EMD produced significant reductions in subjective distress (SUD scores)
- Effects maintained at 3-month follow-up
- Treatment effective across diverse trauma types (sexual abuse, combat, emotional abuse)
- The procedure required simultaneous dual attention: traumatic memory + bilateral eye movements
- Established the foundational protocol later elaborated into full EMDR therapy

## Relevance to Aureonoise

This is the origin paper for bilateral stimulation as a therapeutic modality. While the 1989 study focused on eye movements, subsequent research extended the bilateral stimulation principle to auditory alternation (tones alternating between ears). This paper established two critical parameters that carry through to Aureonoise: (1) the dual-attention requirement (simultaneous memory access + bilateral task), and (2) the rapid onset of therapeutic effect (single session). Aureonoise's bilateral oscillator implements the auditory version of what Shapiro originally demonstrated with eye movements.
