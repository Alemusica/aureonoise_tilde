## Abstract

Building on the Iaccarino 2016 visual-only study (paper 018), the authors extended gamma entrainment using sensory stimulation (GENUS) to the auditory modality. They designed auditory tone stimulation that drove 40 Hz gamma frequency neural activity in auditory cortex and hippocampal CA1. Seven days of auditory GENUS improved spatial and recognition memory and reduced amyloid in auditory cortex and hippocampus of 5XFAD Alzheimer's disease mouse models. Combined audiovisual stimulation (but not either modality alone) produced microglial-clustering responses and decreased amyloid in medial prefrontal cortex. Whole-brain analysis revealed widespread reduction of amyloid plaques throughout the neocortex after multi-sensory GENUS.

## Key Findings
- **Auditory 40 Hz stimulation alone** drives gamma in auditory cortex AND hippocampus (not just sensory cortex)
- Auditory GENUS improved memory in 5XFAD mice after 7 days of treatment
- Auditory GENUS reduced amyloid in auditory cortex and hippocampus
- **Combined audiovisual** stimulation was more effective than either modality alone — produced effects across entire neocortex
- Microglial clustering response (phagocytic morphology) required combined stimulation
- Effect pathway: 40 Hz tones -> auditory cortex entrainment -> hippocampal gamma -> microglia activation -> amyloid clearance

## Relevance to Aureonoise

This is the critical paper validating that AUDITORY gamma stimulation (not just visual flicker) produces neuroprotective effects. It directly justifies Aureonoise's 40 Hz isochronic tone mode. The finding that auditory stimulation reaches the hippocampus (memory center) is particularly important — it means headphone-based 40 Hz delivery can target the brain regions most affected in Alzheimer's. The superiority of combined sensory stimulation suggests Aureonoise could be paired with visual 40 Hz devices for enhanced effect.
