# 038 — Neural Correlates of Shamanic Trance

## Metadata
- **Title:** Neural Correlates of the Shamanic State of Consciousness
- **Authors:** Emma R Huels, Hyoungkyu Kim, UnCheol Lee, Tarik Bel-Bahar, Angelo V Colmenero, Amanda Nelson, Stefanie Blain-Moraes, George A Mashour, Richard E Harris
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fnhum.2021.610466
- **PMC:** PMC8012721
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8012721/

## Abstract
The study investigated neural correlates of shamanic trance using high-density EEG in 24 experienced shamanic practitioners and 24 matched controls during rest, shamanic drumming, and classical music, with altered states assessment. Practitioners showed significantly greater changes across consciousness domains, with scores comparable to or exceeding psychedelic drug effects.

## Key Findings
- Shamanic practitioners entered altered states matching or exceeding psychedelic drug responses
- Increased gamma oscillations during drumming correlated with elementary visual alterations
- Decreased neural signal diversity in gamma band inversely correlated with insightfulness
- Increased brain network criticality during trance (heightened susceptibility to perturbations)
- Decreased low alpha and increased low beta connectivity during trance
- Shamanic trance and psychedelic states are phenomenologically similar but neurobiologically distinct

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Key finding: rhythmic auditory stimulation can produce altered states comparable to psychedelic drugs, but through DIFFERENT neural mechanisms.
