# 026 — Frequencies That Never Synchronize: The Golden Mean and Resting EEG

## Metadata
- **Title:** When frequencies never synchronize: the golden mean and the resting EEG
- **Authors:** Belinda Pletzer, Hubert Kerschbaum, Wolfgang Klimesch
- **Year:** 2010
- **Journal:** Brain Research
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.brainres.2010.03.074
- **PubMed:** 20350536
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0006899310007092

## Abstract
The researchers demonstrate that EEG frequency bands follow a geometric series with a ratio of 1.618 (the golden mean). Synchronization of the excitatory phases of two oscillations with frequencies f1 and f2 is impossible (in a mathematical sense) when their ratio equals the golden mean, because their excitatory phases never meet. This mathematical property suggests the golden ratio generates an optimally desynchronized resting state, while allowing occasional irregular phase coupling that enables transitions from rest to activity.

## Key Findings
- EEG frequency bands follow a geometric series with ratio phi = 1.618
- When f1/f2 = phi, the excitatory phases NEVER synchronize (mathematical impossibility)
- The golden ratio creates an optimally desynchronized resting state
- Irregular (non-periodic) phase coupling at phi ratios enables rest-to-activity transitions
- This explains why EEG bands are spaced at phi ratios: maximum independence + occasional coupling

## Relevance to Aureonoise
THIS IS THE MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION. Phi-ratio frequencies cannot synchronize — this is a mathematical fact, not a tendency. The brain exploits this to maintain independent processing channels at rest. When Aureonoise uses phi-ratio frequency relationships, it creates audio channels that are maximally independent (no interference) but can occasionally couple (enabling integration). This is the opposite of harmonic relationships (which force synchronization). Aureonoise's design should use phi ratios for resting/integration states and harmonic ratios for entrainment/activation states.
