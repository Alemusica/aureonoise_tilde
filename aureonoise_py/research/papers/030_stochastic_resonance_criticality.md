# 030 — Stochastic Resonance at Criticality in Human Cortex

## Metadata
- **Title:** Stochastic resonance at criticality in a network model of the human cortex
- **Authors:** Bertha Vazquez-Rodriguez, Andrea Avena-Koenigsberger, Olaf Sporns, Alessandra Griffa, Patric Hagmann, Hernan Larralde
- **Year:** 2017
- **Journal:** Scientific Reports
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-017-13400-5
- **PubMed:** 29026142
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-13400-5

## Abstract
The research examines how noise can enhance signal transmission in a brain network model. Researchers implemented a dynamic model on an anatomical connectome to investigate signal fidelity at varying noise levels. They found that non-zero levels of noise enhance the similarity between the input signal and the signal that has traveled through the system. Notably, optimal noise levels emerge at critical parameter values where the system demonstrates enhanced information transmission capability. The multiplicity of critical points in the model allows it to adapt to different noise situations and remain at criticality.

## Key Findings
- Non-zero noise levels enhance signal transmission fidelity in brain networks
- Optimal noise levels emerge at critical points (edge of chaos)
- Multiple critical points allow adaptive noise optimization
- The brain exploits stochastic resonance at criticality for information transmission
- The effect is network-dependent — anatomy shapes optimal noise levels
