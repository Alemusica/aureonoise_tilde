# 063 — Slezin: Prayer State and 3 Hz Delta (Fourth Brain State)

## Metadata
- **Title:** (No single English-language peer-reviewed paper identified; research published primarily in Russian journals and conference proceedings)
- **Researcher:** Professor Valery Borisovich Slezin, Doctor of Biological Sciences, Candidate of Medical Sciences
- **Institution:** Laboratory of Neuro- and Psychophysiology, V.M. Bekhterev Psychoneurological Research Institute, St. Petersburg
- **Period:** 1990s-2000s
- **ResearchGate:** https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/V-B-Slezin-68374978
- **Related publication:** Slezin VB et al., "Multifractal analysis as an aid in the diagnostics of mental disorders," Nordic Journal of Psychiatry, 2007

## Research Summary

Slezin conducted over 200 EEG measurements on subjects from multiple religious confessions (Orthodox, Catholic, Muslim) and various social backgrounds, including 7 students of a Spiritual Seminary and Academy. He recorded EEG during prayer and discovered what he termed the "fourth brain state."

Before Slezin, neuroscience recognized three brain states:
1. **Wakefulness** (beta/alpha dominant)
2. **Fast sleep / REM** (theta/beta)
3. **Slow sleep / NREM** (delta dominant, unconscious)

Slezin discovered: **"Prayer Wakefulness"** (molitesvennoe bodrstvovanie) — delta dominant BUT conscious.

## Key Findings
- During deep prayer, brain rhythm slows to **2-3 Hz delta**
- Delta amplitude is extremely low — approaching a flat line
- This EEG pattern is normally seen **only in infants aged 0-3 months**
- The cerebral cortex appears "disconnected" but the person is conscious and perceiving
- The effect was **identical regardless of religion** (cross-confessional)
- Oxygen consumption drops approximately 20% during this state
- **Hypothesis (unverified):** During 3 Hz delta prayer state, pathological connections between neurons are destroyed

## Scientific Status

**Tier B — Measured and replicated across 200+ subjects, but limited to Russian publication venues.** The EEG measurements themselves are straightforward and well-documented. The cross-confessional consistency strengthens the finding. The claim that pathological neural connections are "destroyed" at 3 Hz is Slezin's hypothesis, not a directly observed mechanism. Important caveat: 3 Hz spike-wave is also the characteristic pattern of absence seizures (petit mal epilepsy, Gibbs et al. 1935), and the relationship between pathological 3 Hz and Slezin's "healing" 3 Hz has not been adequately explored.
