# 016 — IIT 3.0

## Metadata
- **Title:** From the Phenomenology to the Mechanisms of Consciousness: Integrated Information Theory 3.0
- **Authors:** Masafumi Oizumi, Larissa Albantakis, Giulio Tononi
- **Year:** 2014
- **Journal:** PLOS Computational Biology
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588
- **URL:** https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588

## Abstract
This paper presents Integrated Information Theory (IIT) of consciousness 3.0, which incorporates several advances over previous formulations. IIT starts from phenomenological axioms: information says that each experience is specific; integration says that it is unified — irreducible to non-interdependent components; exclusion says that it has unique borders and a particular spatio-temporal grain. These axioms are formalized into postulates that prescribe how physical mechanisms, such as neurons or logic gates, must be configured to generate experience. The postulates are used to define intrinsic information as 'differences that make a difference' within a system, and integrated information as information specified by a whole that cannot be reduced to that specified by its parts. By applying the postulates both at the level of individual mechanisms and at the level of systems of mechanisms, IIT arrives at an identity: an experience is a maximally irreducible conceptual structure (MICS), and the set of elements that generates it constitutes a complex.

## Key Findings
- Experience = maximally irreducible conceptual structure (MICS)
- Systems may contain major and non-overlapping minor complexes
- Anatomical connectivity influences conscious structures
- Simple systems can be minimally conscious; complex systems can lack consciousness
- Feed-forward systems can be functional "zombies" — unconscious despite equivalent function

## Relevance to Aureonoise
IIT 3.0 formalizes that consciousness requires irreducibility — the whole must generate more information than its parts. Aureonoise's multi-frequency architecture (phi-ratio relationships between bands) is designed to maximize irreducibility by creating coupling patterns that cannot be decomposed into independent components. The exclusion postulate (unique spatio-temporal grain) informs why bilateral presentation matters: it creates a spatial integration that monaural presentation cannot.
