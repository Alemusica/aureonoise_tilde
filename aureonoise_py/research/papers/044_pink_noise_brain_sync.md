# 044 — Pink Noise: Brain Synchronization and Sleep

## Metadata
- **Title:** Pink noise: effect on complexity synchronization of brain activity and sleep consolidation
- **Authors:** Junhong Zhou, Dongdong Liu, Xin Li, Jing Ma, Jue Zhang, Jing Fang
- **Year:** 2012
- **Journal:** Journal of Theoretical Biology
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.04.006
- **PubMed:** 22726808
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022519312001798

## Abstract
The researchers hypothesized that steady pink noise could alter brain activity complexity and enhance sleep stability. In their brain synchronization test with 6 subjects, they alternated 10-minute quiet periods with 10-minute noise exposure while recording EEG signals. Fractal dimension analysis revealed that the complexity of the EEG signals decreased with the introduction of the pink noise exposure, indicating synchronized brain waves. For sleep quality assessment, 40 nocturnal sleep participants and 10 nap test participants each completed two experimental sessions. Results demonstrated significant enhancement in the percentage of stable sleep time in the noise-exposed group compared to controls.

## Key Findings
- Pink noise decreases EEG complexity (increased synchronization)
- Pink noise significantly enhances percentage of stable sleep time
- Fractal dimension of EEG decreases with pink noise exposure
- Effect observed in both nocturnal sleep and nap conditions
- Pink noise acts as a brain synchronizer, not just a masking noise
