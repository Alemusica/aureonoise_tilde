# 032 — Transcranial Random Noise Stimulation and Stochastic Resonance

## Metadata
- **Title:** Transcranial Random Noise Stimulation into the Visual Cortex Enhances Perception via Stochastic Resonance
- **Authors:** Onno van der Groen, Nicole Wenderoth
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Journal of Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4519-15.2016
- **PubMed:** 27170126
- **URL:** https://www.jneurosci.org/content/36/19/5289

## Abstract
The research demonstrates that random electrical noise applied to visual cortex enhances weak signal detection through stochastic resonance. When participants received transcranial random noise stimulation (tRNS) at optimal intensity levels, visual detection accuracy improved by approximately 9.7%. This improvement matched the benefit observed when noise was added directly to visual stimuli (11.2% improvement).

## Key Findings
- Random noise stimulation enhances weak signal detection by ~10%
- Effect follows the inverted-U pattern characteristic of stochastic resonance
- Subthreshold stimuli benefit significantly more than suprathreshold ones
- External noise to the cortex is functionally equivalent to noise added to the stimulus
- SR could enhance various cognitive functions beyond perception

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Demonstrates that externally applied noise enhances cortical signal detection through stochastic resonance. While this study used electrical stimulation, the principle applies to auditory noise: Aureonoise's noise generators provide the stochastic input that enhances the brain's sensitivity to weak endogenous signals (such as theta/alpha oscillations). The finding that subthreshold stimuli benefit most is critical — Aureonoise should deliver noise at levels that support but do not overwhelm endogenous neural activity.
