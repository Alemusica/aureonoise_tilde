# 058 — Wahbeh 2007: Binaural Beat Technology Pilot Study

## Metadata
- **Title:** Binaural beat technology in humans: a pilot study to assess neuropsychologic, physiologic, and electroencephalographic effects
- **Authors:** Helane Wahbeh, Carlo Calabrese, Heather Zwickey, Dan Zajdel
- **Year:** 2007
- **Journal:** Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine
- **Volume/Pages:** 13(2):199-206
- **DOI:** 10.1089/acm.2006.6201
- **PubMed:** 17388762
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17388762/

## Abstract

A randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled crossover pilot study in 4 healthy adults. Subjects were randomized to experimental auditory stimulus of 30 minutes of 7 Hz binaural beat (carrier frequencies: 133 Hz left, 140 Hz right) with an overlay of pink noise resembling rain, or control stimuli of the same pink noise overlay without the binaural beat carrier frequencies. Neuropsychologic measures included State Trait Anxiety Inventory, Profile of Mood States, Rey Auditory Verbal List Test, Stroop Test, and Controlled Oral Word Association Test. EEG spectral and coherence analysis was performed.

Results: No significant differences between experimental and control conditions in any EEG measures. The experimental condition showed increased depression subscale scores (p=0.02) and worse immediate verbal recall compared to control. The study did not support steady-state EEG entrainment from binaural beats.

## Key Findings
- **No EEG entrainment** at 7 Hz was observed from binaural beats
- Binaural beats embedded in pink noise produced NO measurable entrainment effect
- Possible negative mood effects (increased depression scores) with beat-in-noise condition
- Small sample size (n=4) limits conclusions but the null EEG finding is consistent with later larger studies
