# 041 — Binaural Beats and Interhemispheric Alpha Coherence

## Metadata
- **Title:** Binaural beats increase interhemispheric alpha-band coherence between auditory cortices
- **Authors:** Marco Solca, Anais Mottaz, Adrian G Guggisberg
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Hearing Research
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.heares.2015.09.011
- **PubMed:** 26541421
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378595515300149

## Abstract
The researchers investigated how binaural beats — an auditory illusion created when two slightly different frequency tones are presented separately to each ear — affect brain synchronization. Using EEG recordings, they found that binaural beats enhanced interhemispheric coherence between the auditory cortices compared to control conditions. Both alpha (10 Hz) and theta (4 Hz) frequency beats increased synchronization specifically at alpha frequencies. However, a follow-up behavioral test showed no lasting effects. The authors concluded that binaural beats strengthen synchronization between brain hemispheres during listening but appear to reflect auditory integration rather than neural entrainment.

## Key Findings
- Binaural beats enhanced interhemispheric coherence between auditory cortices
- Both alpha and theta beat frequencies increased alpha-band synchronization
- Effect present during listening but no lasting behavioral effects
- The effect likely reflects auditory integration, not neural entrainment
- Binaural beats are a real neural phenomenon but with limited therapeutic utility

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Important negative finding: binaural beats produce measurable neural effects (interhemispheric alpha coherence) but no lasting behavioral changes. This constrains Aureonoise's approach — pure binaural beats are insufficient for therapeutic effects. Aureonoise's multi-mechanism approach (bilateral alternation + noise + cross-frequency coupling) is designed to go beyond what binaural beats alone can achieve. The interhemispheric coherence finding validates the bilateral pathway but shows it needs more than frequency difference to produce lasting change.
