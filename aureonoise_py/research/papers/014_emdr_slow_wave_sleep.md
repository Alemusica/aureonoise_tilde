# 014 — EMDR and Slow Wave Sleep

## Metadata
- **Title:** Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing and Slow Wave Sleep: A Putative Mechanism of Action
- **Authors:** Marco Pagani, Benedikt L Amann, Ramon Landin-Romero, Sara Carletto
- **Year:** 2017
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01935
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01935/full

## Abstract
EMDR is considered highly efficacious for the treatment of Post-traumatic Stress Disorder and has proved to be a valid treatment approach with a wide range of applications. However, EMDR's mechanisms of action is not yet fully understood. This paper discusses a conjecture which focuses on the similarity between the delta waves recorded by electroencephalography during Slow Wave Sleep (SWS) and those registered upon typical EMDR bilateral stimulation (eye movements or alternate tapping) during recurrent distressing memories of an emotionally traumatic event. SWS appears to have a key role in memory consolidation and in the reorganization of distant functional networks, as well as Eye Movements seem to reduce traumatic episodic memory and favor the reconsolidation of new associated information.

## Key Findings
- Delta wave patterns during EMDR bilateral stimulation resemble those in slow wave sleep
- SWS plays a key role in memory consolidation and network reorganization
- EMDR may exploit the same neurophysiological mechanism as SWS for memory processing
- Eye movements reduce traumatic episodic memory and support reconsolidation

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The EMDR-SWS connection suggests that bilateral stimulation induces delta-like brain states associated with memory consolidation. Aureonoise could exploit this by incorporating delta-frequency modulation during bilateral stimulation modes. This bridges the EMDR literature with the memory reconsolidation literature (paper 028-029). The implication: Aureonoise's therapeutic sessions might be most effective when they mimic the temporal dynamics of SWS (slow oscillations with embedded faster rhythms).
