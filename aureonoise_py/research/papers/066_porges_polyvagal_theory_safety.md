## Abstract

Polyvagal Theory identifies three phylogenetically ordered neural circuits mediating autonomic state: (1) ventral vagal complex (social engagement, safety), (2) sympathetic nervous system (fight/flight, danger), and (3) dorsal vagal complex (freeze/collapse, life threat). The theory proposes that the autonomic nervous system functions as a neural platform for social behavior, with the perception of safety being the critical variable. The myelinated ventral vagal pathway supports social engagement behaviors (facial expression, vocalization, listening) and calms the body through vagal brake mechanisms. The middle ear muscles, controlled by cranial nerves V and VII, tune auditory perception to prioritize human voice frequencies (prosodic range, ~500-4000 Hz) when in a safe state.

## Key Findings
- **Three-circuit model:** Ventral vagal (safety/social), sympathetic (danger), dorsal vagal (life threat)
- Safety is not the absence of threat — it requires active neural detection ("neuroception")
- **Middle ear muscle function:** Cranial nerves V and VII control muscles that attenuate low-frequency background noise, enhancing perception of human voice frequencies
- In trauma/stress, middle ear muscles disengage, shifting auditory tuning toward predator/threat frequencies
- The Safe and Sound Protocol (SSP) uses filtered music emphasizing 500-4000 Hz to retrain middle ear muscles
- **SSP clinical evidence:** DoD-funded $3.8M study for PTSD; 5 hours total (30-min sessions over 10 days)

## Relevance to Aureonoise

Porges' polyvagal theory directly informs Aureonoise's spectral shaping for safety. The prosodic frequency range (500-4000 Hz) should be emphasized in presets targeting anxiety and PTSD, paralleling the SSP mechanism. The theory also explains WHY bilateral stimulation works at the autonomic level: the ventral vagal complex integrates auditory input with autonomic regulation. Aureonoise's spectral tilt parameter and noise color selection should consider the prosodic range as a "safety band" — maintaining adequate energy in 500-4000 Hz while the rest of the spectrum is shaped for other therapeutic targets.
