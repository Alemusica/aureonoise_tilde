# 004 — Relationship Between Alpha Rhythm and DMN

## Metadata
- **Title:** Relationship Between Alpha Rhythm and the Default Mode Network: An EEG-fMRI Study
- **Authors:** Anthony D Bowman, Joseph C Griffis, Kristina M Visscher, Allan C Dobbins, Timothy J Gawne, Mark W DiFrancesco, Jerzy P Szaflarski
- **Year:** 2017
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Neurophysiology
- **DOI:** 10.1097/WNP.0000000000000411
- **PMC:** PMC8428580
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8428580/

## Abstract
Reports of the relationship between the default mode network (DMN) and alpha power are conflicting. Our goal was to assess this relationship by analyzing concurrently obtained EEG/functional MRI data using hypothesis-independent methods. We collected functional MRI and EEG data during eyes-closed rest in 20 participants aged 19 to 37 (10 females) and performed independent component analysis on the functional MRI data and a Hamming-windowed fast Fourier transform on the EEG data. We correlated functional MRI fluctuations in the DMN with alpha power. Of the six independent components found to have significant relationships with alpha, four contained DMN-associated regions: One independent component was positively correlated with alpha power, whereas all others were negatively correlated. Furthermore, two independent components with opposite relationships with alpha had overlapping voxels in the medial prefrontal cortex and posterior cingulate cortex, suggesting that subpopulations of neurons within these classic nodes within the DMN may have different relationships to alpha power. Different parts of the DMN exhibit divergent relationships to alpha power.

## Key Findings
- Four of six significant EEG-fMRI components contained DMN regions
- Subpopulations within mPFC and PCC show opposite relationships to alpha power
- The DMN-alpha relationship is not uniform — it is subnetwork-dependent
- Both positive and negative correlations coexist within classic DMN hubs

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The alpha-DMN relationship is not monolithic. Different DMN subnetworks respond differently to alpha. This means Aureonoise cannot assume a single alpha frequency will uniformly affect the entire DMN. The implication: individual alpha frequency (IAF) matters, and spatial presentation (bilateral vs. midline) may differentially activate DMN subcomponents.
