# 046 — Theta Rhythm of the Hippocampus

## Metadata
- **Title:** The Theta Rhythm of the Hippocampus: From Neuronal and Circuit Mechanisms to Behavior
- **Authors:** Angel Nunez, Washington Buno
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fncel.2021.649262
- **PMC:** PMC7970048
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970048/

## Abstract
This review focuses on the neuronal and circuit mechanisms involved in the generation of the theta rhythm and of its participation in behavior. Data have accumulated indicating that theta arises from interactions between medial septum-diagonal band of Broca (MS-DbB) and intra-hippocampal circuits. The intrinsic properties of MS-DbB and hippocampal neurons have also been shown to play a key role in theta generation. A growing number of studies suggest that theta may represent a timing mechanism to temporally organize movement sequences, memory encoding, or planned trajectories for spatial navigation. To accomplish those functions, theta and gamma oscillations interact during the awake state and REM sleep, which are considered to be critical for learning and memory processes.

## Key Findings
- Theta depends on rhythmic activity from medial septum neurons
- GABAergic inputs regulate hippocampal oscillations through rhythmic disinhibition
- Intrinsic pyramidal neuron properties generate slow spikes contributing to theta
- Theta-gamma coupling is vital for memory processes
- Theta-gamma coupling declines in Alzheimer's disease pathology

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Hippocampal theta is the master clock for memory encoding and retrieval. Aureonoise's theta mode targets this system.
