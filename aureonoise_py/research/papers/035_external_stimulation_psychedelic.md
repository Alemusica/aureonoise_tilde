# 035 — External Stimulation and Psychedelic Neurodynamics

## Metadata
- **Title:** Effects of External Stimulation on Psychedelic State Neurodynamics
- **Authors:** Pedro A M Mediano, Fernando E Rosas, Christopher Timmermann, Leor Roseman, David J Nutt, Amanda Feilding, Mendel Kaelen, Morten L Kringelbach, Adam B Barrett, Anil K Seth, Suresh Muthukumaraswamy, Daniel Bor, Robin L Carhart-Harris
- **Year:** 2024

- **DOI:** 10.1021/acschemneuro.3c00289
- **PMC:** PMC10853937
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10853937/

## Abstract
The study examined how brain entropy changes during LSD versus placebo under different environmental conditions. Researchers tested 20 participants across four settings: eyes closed, listening to music with eyes closed, eyes open, and watching video. Results indicated that brain entropy increases with LSD under all experimental conditions but showed largest changes when subjects have their eyes closed. The relationship between entropy and subjective experience is disrupted when participants are viewing a video.

## Key Findings
- External stimuli reduce LSD's entropy-enhancing neurological effect
- Eyes-closed conditions produce stronger drug-related brain changes
- Neural-psychological correlations weaken significantly during video viewing
- Environmental setting substantially modulates psychedelic neurodynamics

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Critical design insight: external sensory input CONSTRAINS brain entropy changes. This means Aureonoise must be carefully designed to not over-specify the auditory environment. For entropy-enhancing modes, the audio should be minimally structured (noise-like) to allow maximum endogenous entropy. For entrainment modes, structured audio is appropriate. The eyes-closed finding validates Aureonoise as an audio-only tool — visual input would reduce the neurodynamic effects. Music listening showed intermediate effects, suggesting Aureonoise's structured-but-non-musical approach may be optimal.
