# 055 — Theta-Alpha Frequency Convergence and Golden Ratio Organization

## Metadata
- **Title:** Golden ratio organization in human EEG is associated with theta-alpha frequency convergence: a multi-dataset validation study
- **Authors:** Andrei Ursachi
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fnhum.2026.1781338
- **URL:** https://doaj.org/article/b260e3a22fd2459989265e7a59fd81b9

## Abstract
The study investigates whether theta-alpha frequency ratios near the golden ratio (phi ~ 1.618) associate with theta-alpha convergence at 8 Hz. Researchers developed the Phi Coupling Index (PCI) measuring spectral frequency ratio proximity, tested across 320 subjects from two datasets. Results showed 80% exhibited phi-organization, with strong association with theta-alpha convergence (r = 0.54, p < 10^-25). The effect replicated across datasets, showed robustness to outliers, and demonstrated phi-specificity. Frontal theta analysis yielded even stronger effects (r = 0.718, p ~ 10^-35), providing evidence against volume conduction artifacts. Sex differences emerged, with females showing stronger associations than males.

## Key Findings
- 80% of subjects demonstrate phi-organization in their EEG
- Strong correlation between Phi Coupling Index and theta-alpha convergence (r = 0.54)
- Effect replicates across independent datasets
- Frontal theta shows even stronger correlation (r = 0.718)
- Sex differences: females show stronger phi-organization than males
- Age-related variations in younger vs older adults
