# 031 — Stochastic Resonance in Sensory Systems and Neural Prosthetics

## Metadata
- **Title:** Stochastic resonance in the sensory systems and its applications in neural prosthetics
- **Authors:** Patrick Matthews, Pratik Raul, Lawrence M Ward, Jeroen J A van Boxtel
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Clinical Neurophysiology
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.clinph.2024.07.004
- **PubMed:** 39047671
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1388245724002025

## Abstract
Noise is generally considered to be detrimental. In the right conditions, however, noise can improve signal detection or information transmission. This phenomenon, termed stochastic resonance, is explored across auditory, sensorimotor, and visual systems. The review examines how SR-based technologies might benefit individuals with sensory impairments. The researchers conclude substantial evidence supports using stochastic resonance in creating sensory prosthetics, though progress varies across different sensory modalities.

## Key Findings
- Stochastic resonance improves signal detection across auditory, visual, and sensorimotor systems
- Optimal noise level follows an inverted-U function (too little or too much noise both impair)
- SR-based technologies show promise for sensory prosthetics
- The auditory system is particularly responsive to stochastic resonance
- Progress varies by sensory modality — auditory SR is the most well-established

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Validates that the auditory system is particularly responsive to stochastic resonance. Aureonoise's noise-based stimulation is not arbitrary — the auditory pathway is optimally suited for SR-based neuromodulation. The inverted-U noise function constrains Aureonoise's noise amplitude: there is an optimal level, and exceeding it degrades performance. The 2024 publication date makes this the most current review of SR in auditory processing.
