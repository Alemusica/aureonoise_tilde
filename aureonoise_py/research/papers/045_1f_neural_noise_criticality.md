# 045 — 1/f Neural Noise and Self-Organized Criticality

## Metadata
- **Title:** Self-organized criticality as a fundamental property of neural systems
- **Authors:** Janina Hesse, Thilo Gross
- **Year:** 2014
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fnsys.2014.00166
- **PMC:** PMC4171833
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4171833/

## Abstract
The neural criticality hypothesis states that the brain may be poised in a critical state at a boundary between different types of dynamics. Theoretical and experimental studies show that critical systems often exhibit optimal computational properties, suggesting the possibility that criticality has been evolutionarily selected as a useful trait for our nervous system. Evidence for criticality has been found in cell cultures, brain slices, and anesthetized animals. Yet, inconsistent results were reported for recordings in awake animals and humans, and current results point to open questions about the exact nature and mechanism of criticality, as well as its functional role.

## Key Findings
- Critical systems exhibit optimal computational properties (maximal dynamic range, information transmission)
- Evidence supports criticality in cultures and slices; inconsistent in awake humans
- Self-organized criticality mechanisms enable systems to maintain criticality through local processes
- Power-law distributions in neuronal avalanches are hallmarks of criticality
- The brain likely self-organizes to a slightly subcritical state rather than perfect criticality

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The brain operates at or near criticality — the edge between order and chaos. 1/f noise (pink noise) is the signature of critical systems. Aureonoise's pink noise mode is not arbitrary: it provides input that matches the brain's own critical dynamics. The slightly subcritical finding (not exactly at criticality) connects to the entropic brain hypothesis (paper 033): normal waking consciousness is slightly below criticality, and various interventions (psychedelics, meditation, Aureonoise) can shift the brain toward or away from this critical point.
