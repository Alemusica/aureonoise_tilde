# 020 — Gamma Entrainment and DMN Connectivity in Dementia

## Metadata
- **Title:** Auditory gamma-band entrainment enhances default mode network connectivity in dementia patients
- **Authors:** Mojtaba Lahijanian, Hamid Aghajan, Zahra Vahabi
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Scientific Reports
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-024-63727-z
- **PMC:** PMC11161471
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161471/

## Abstract
Dementia, and in particular Alzheimer's disease (AD), can be characterized by disrupted functional connectivity in the brain caused by beta-amyloid deposition in neural links. Non-pharmaceutical treatments for dementia have recently explored interventions involving the stimulation of neuronal populations in the gamma band. Entrainment, a newly proposed non-invasive sensory stimulation method, has shown promise in improving cognitive functions in dementia patients. This study investigates the effectiveness of entrainment in terms of promoting neural synchrony and spatial connectivity across the cortex. EEG signals were recorded during a 40 Hz auditory entrainment session conducted with a group of elderly participants with dementia. Phase locking value (PLV) between different intraregional and interregional sites was examined.

## Key Findings
- 40 Hz auditory entrainment significantly increases synchrony between frontal and parietal regions
- Long-range frontoparietal connectivity showed the most significant enhancement
- Entrainment response correlated strongly with synchronization improvement (r = 0.894)
- Source localization revealed entrained activity in DMN regions: superior parietal lobe, precuneus, superior frontal

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Direct validation that AUDITORY 40 Hz entrainment enhances DMN connectivity in dementia patients. This bridges the gamma literature (papers 018-019, mouse models) to human clinical populations. The frontoparietal DMN connectivity enhancement links gamma stimulation to the same network targeted by alpha stimulation (papers 001-006), suggesting Aureonoise's multi-frequency approach can target DMN through multiple frequency channels simultaneously.
