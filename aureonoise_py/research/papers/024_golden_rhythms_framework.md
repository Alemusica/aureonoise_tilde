## Abstract
While brain rhythms appear fundamental to brain function, why brain rhythms consistently organize into the small set of discrete frequency bands observed remains unknown. Here we propose that rhythms separated by factors of the golden ratio (phi = (1+sqrt(5))/2) optimally support segregation and cross-frequency integration of information transmission in the brain. Organized by the golden ratio, pairs of transient rhythms support multiplexing by reducing interference between separate communication channels, and triplets of transient rhythms support integration of signals to establish a hierarchy of cross-frequency interactions. We illustrate this framework in simulation and apply this framework to propose four hypotheses.

## Key Findings
- Brain rhythms organized by golden ratio spacing enable selective cross-frequency coupling
- Triplets of golden rhythms possess the lowest resonance order, supporting strongest cross-frequency interactions
- Golden ratio organization requires fewer distinct rhythmic mechanisms than alternatives (Euler's number, factor-of-2)
- Empirical data from hippocampal recordings show frequency pairs often separated by phi or phi-squared factors
