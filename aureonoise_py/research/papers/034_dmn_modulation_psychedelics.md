## Abstract
Psychedelics are a unique class of drug that commonly produce vivid hallucinations as well as profound psychological and mystical experiences. A grouping of interconnected brain regions characterized by increased temporal coherence at rest have been termed the Default Mode Network (DMN). Several studies have investigated how psychedelics modulate this network. Across psychedelics there is consistent acute disruption in resting state connectivity within the DMN and increased functional connectivity between canonical resting-state networks.

## Key Findings
- Classical psychedelics consistently disrupt resting-state connectivity within the DMN
- Psychedelics increase functional connectivity BETWEEN different brain networks
- Within-network connectivity decreases while between-network connectivity increases
- DMN modulation correlates with altered consciousness, ego dissolution, and therapeutic outcomes
- REBUS model proposes DMN disruption facilitates cognitive flexibility and belief updating

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Psychedelics modulate the DMN in the opposite direction from alpha stimulation: they disrupt it rather than enhance it. This informs Aureonoise's mode design: alpha/calming modes strengthen DMN (papers 001-006), while entropy/complexity modes could mimic the psychedelic pattern of reduced within-DMN and increased between-network connectivity. The REBUS (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) model is relevant — Aureonoise's complex modes could promote belief updating and cognitive flexibility without pharmacological intervention.
