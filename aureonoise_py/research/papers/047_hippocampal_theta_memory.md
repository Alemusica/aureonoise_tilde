# 047 — Hippocampal Theta and Memory: Phase-Specific Enhancement

## Metadata
- **Title:** Enhancement of encoding and retrieval functions through theta phase-specific manipulation of hippocampus
- **Authors:** Joshua H Siegle, Matthew A Wilson
- **Year:** 2014
- **Journal:** eLife
- **DOI:** 10.7554/eLife.03061
- **PMC:** PMC4384761
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4384761/

## Abstract
Assessing the behavioral relevance of the hippocampal theta rhythm has proven difficult, due to a shortage of experiments that selectively manipulate phase-specific information processing. Using closed-loop stimulation, we triggered inhibition of dorsal CA1 at specific phases of the endogenous theta rhythm in freely behaving mice. This intervention enhanced performance on a spatial navigation task that requires the encoding and retrieval of information related to reward location on every trial.

## Key Findings
- Phase-specific hippocampal inhibition improved spatial task performance
- Peak theta stimulation enhanced ENCODING
- Trough theta stimulation enhanced RETRIEVAL
- Double-dissociation: encoding and retrieval are locked to different theta phases
- Results support computational models of distinct theta phases for encoding vs retrieval

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The peak/trough dissociation is a critical parameter for Aureonoise. Theta phase determines whether the brain is encoding or retrieving. If Aureonoise can bias stimulation toward specific theta phases, it could selectively enhance encoding (for learning modes) or retrieval (for memory reconsolidation/EMDR modes). This connects to paper 028: reconsolidation requires retrieval first, so trough-phase stimulation could open the reconsolidation window, followed by encoding-phase stimulation for reintegration.
