## Key Concepts

### Error Detector (Detektor Oshibok) — 1968
- Populations of neurons that respond to erroneous performance
- Located primarily in the **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)** and subcortical structures (globus pallidus, thalamic nuclei)
- Functions by comparing current state against an internal "matrix" (plan-template stored in memory)
- Normal function: **protective** — catches deviations from correct patterns
- Operates unconsciously — below awareness

### Stable Pathological State (Ustoichivoe Patologicheskoe Sostoianie / UPS)
- The brain seeks **stable states**, whether healthy or pathological
- After destabilization due to disease, the brain can adopt a pathological state as its new "normal"
- Even when the original cause disappears, the organism cannot self-exit this state
- **The error detector becomes the enforcer of pathology** — it detects attempts to return to health as "errors" and corrects back to the pathological pattern
- All therapeutic interventions are fought by the error detector
- This explains chronic neurological conditions that resist treatment

### Super-Capabilities and Prohibitions (Sverkhvozmozhnosti i Zaprety)
- The brain has latent **super-capabilities** normally suppressed by **"prohibitions"**
- Under specific conditions (training, altered states, pathological disinhibition), super-capabilities emerge
- PET studies: creative thinking activates **prefrontal cortical areas of BOTH hemispheres** (Brodmann's fields 8-11 and 45-47)

## Relevance to Aureonoise

Bekhtereva's UPS theory provides the theoretical framework for Aureonoise's deepest therapeutic ambition: **dissolution of stable pathological states.** The mechanism:
1. Identify the error detector's enforcement pattern (reflected in persistent coherence asymmetries)
2. Gradually shift the "matrix" by presenting phi-ratio relationships as a new stable reference
3. Use 3 Hz delta state (Slezin, paper 063) to temporarily disable the error detector, creating a reorganization window
4. Bilateral activation during creativity (both hemispheres, prefrontal) maps to the bilateral oscillator — creating conditions that mirror creative-state brain activity
