# 013 — EMDR Mechanisms Systematic Review

## Metadata
- **Title:** How Does Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing Therapy Work? A Systematic Review on Suggested Mechanisms of Action
- **Authors:** Ramon Landin-Romero, Ana Moreno-Alcazar, Marco Pagani, Benedikt L Amann
- **Year:** 2018
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01395
- **PMC:** PMC6106867
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6106867/

## Abstract
Eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) is an innovative, evidence-based and effective psychotherapy for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). As with other psychotherapies, the effectiveness of EMDR contrasts with a limited knowledge of its underlying mechanism of action. Despite the increasing number of studies published in recent years, the research into the mechanisms underlying EMDR therapy is still in its infancy.

## Key Findings
- 87 studies classified into: psychological (32), psychophysiological (18), neurobiological (37) models
- Reasonable empirical support for the working memory hypothesis
- Physiological changes during successful EMDR therapy documented
- Emerging neuroimaging evidence for brain correlates of EMDR effects
- Research into mechanisms remains in its infancy despite clinical efficacy

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The working memory hypothesis suggests bilateral stimulation competes for working memory resources during trauma recall, reducing emotional intensity. This informs Aureonoise's approach: the auditory stimulation should be cognitively engaging enough to tax working memory but not so complex as to be distracting. The multi-mechanism finding supports Aureonoise's multi-parameter approach (frequency, timing, spatial) rather than targeting a single mechanism.
