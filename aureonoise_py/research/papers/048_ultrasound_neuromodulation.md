# 048 — Ultrasound Neuromodulation Review

## Metadata
- **Title:** Ultrasound Neuromodulation: A Review of Results, Mechanisms and Safety
- **Authors:** Joseph Blackmore, Shamit Shrivastava, Jerome Sallet, Chris R Butler, Robin O Cleveland
- **Year:** 2019
- **Journal:** Ultrasound in Medicine & Biology
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ultrasmedbio.2018.12.015
- **PMC:** PMC6996285
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6996285/

## Abstract
Ultrasonic neuromodulation is a rapidly growing field, in which low-intensity ultrasound is delivered to nervous system tissue, resulting in transient modulation of neural activity. This review summarizes the findings in the central and peripheral nervous systems from mechanistic studies in cell culture to cognitive behavioral studies in humans. The mechanisms by which ultrasound mechanically interacts with neurons and could affect firing are presented. An in-depth safety assessment of current studies shows that parameters for the human studies fall within the safety envelope for ultrasound imaging.

## Key Findings
- Focused ultrasound modulates neural activity across cell cultures, animal models, and humans
- Mechanism is mechanical (acoustic radiation force), not thermal
- Safety parameters fall within diagnostic ultrasound guidelines
- Spatial targeting superior to transcranial magnetic/electrical stimulation
- Peripheral nerve stimulation likely involves cavitation
- Human studies confirm cognitive and sensory modulation

## Relevance to Aureonoise
While Aureonoise uses audible frequencies rather than ultrasound, this paper establishes that mechanical wave energy can modulate neural activity. The mechanism (acoustic radiation force at the cellular level) extends to audible sound at higher intensities. More relevantly, the review validates non-invasive wave-based neuromodulation as a safe and effective approach, supporting Aureonoise's fundamental premise.
