# 008 — Role of Corpus Callosum in Interhemispheric Transfer

## Metadata
- **Title:** The role of the corpus callosum in interhemispheric transfer of information: excitation or inhibition?
- **Authors:** Jason S Bloom, George W Hynd
- **Year:** 2005
- **Journal:** Neuropsychology Review
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11065-005-6252-y
- **PubMed:** 16211466
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11065-005-6252-y

## Abstract
The paper examines the fundamental question: does the corpus callosum serve to communicate an inhibitory or excitatory influence on the contralateral hemisphere? The authors explore how the interhemispheric connection manifests functionally, reviewing evidence that addresses whether callosal communication is primarily inhibitory or excitatory in nature.

## Key Findings
- The corpus callosum serves both excitatory and inhibitory functions
- The nature of callosal transfer depends on the specific task and brain region involved
- Interhemispheric transfer enables both cooperation (excitation) and specialization (inhibition)
- Callosal function is not unitary but context-dependent

## Relevance to Aureonoise
The corpus callosum is the primary pathway for bilateral stimulation effects. Understanding that it serves both excitatory and inhibitory functions is critical for Aureonoise's bilateral presentation design. Alternating bilateral stimulation (as in EMDR) exploits the callosal pathway to create interhemispheric communication patterns. The dual nature (excitation/inhibition) explains why bilateral audio can both activate (gamma entrainment) and calm (alpha synchronization) depending on parameters.
