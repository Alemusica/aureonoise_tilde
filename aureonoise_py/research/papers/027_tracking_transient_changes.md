# 027 — Tracking Transient Changes: Harmonic Theta-Alpha Relationships

## Metadata
- **Title:** Tracking Transient Changes in the Neural Frequency Architecture: Harmonic Relationships between Theta and Alpha Peaks Facilitate Cognitive Performance
- **Authors:** J Rodriguez-Larios, K Alaerts
- **Year:** 2019
- **Journal:** Journal of Neuroscience
- **DOI:** 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2919-18.2019
- **PubMed:** 31175211
- **URL:** https://www.jneurosci.org/content/39/32/6291

## Abstract
The research examined whether oscillations at different frequencies can synchronize when their peak frequencies maintain harmonic relationships (e.g., f2 = f1/2). The study specifically explored whether the transient occurrence of harmonic cross-frequency relationships between theta and alpha frequencies supports improved cognitive performance.

## Key Findings
- Transient harmonic relationships between theta and alpha peaks facilitate cognitive performance
- When theta and alpha transiently achieve harmonic ratios, cross-frequency coupling increases
- These harmonic windows are transient — they appear and disappear dynamically
- Cognitive performance improves during harmonic windows

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Complements papers 024-026. The brain dynamically shifts between phi-ratio (desynchronized, resting) and harmonic (synchronized, active) relationships. Aureonoise could implement this dynamic: sessions that shift between phi-ratio spacing (promoting rest/integration) and harmonic spacing (promoting active processing/entrainment). The transient nature of harmonic windows suggests Aureonoise should not maintain constant frequency ratios but should allow the relationships to evolve over time.
