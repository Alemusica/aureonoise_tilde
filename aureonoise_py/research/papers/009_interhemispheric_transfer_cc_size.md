# 009 — Interhemispheric Transfer and Corpus Callosum Size

## Metadata
- **Title:** Interhemispheric transfer time and corpus callosum size
- **Authors:** L Jancke, H Steinmetz
- **Year:** 1994
- **Journal:** Neuroreport
- **DOI:** 10.1097/00001756-199411000-00043
- **PubMed:** 7881064
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7881064/

## Abstract
We examined the relationship between the size of five mid-sagittal corpus callosum subareas (measured with in vivo magnetic resonance morphometry) and hand reaction times at dichotic consonant-vowel monitoring in fifty healthy young adults. Based on the reaction times, interhemispheric transfer times between auditory and motor brain areas were calculated and related to anterior and posterior corpus callosum subarea measurements. We found no relationship between the size of callosal subareas and auditory or motor interhemispheric transfer times. We did, however, find a significant correlation between mean reaction time and total corpus callosum size. Our results suggest that normal variation in callosal size or shape is not related to individual differences in auditory lateralization. Instead, callosal size may be associated with speed of information processing.

## Key Findings
- No correlation between callosal subarea size and interhemispheric transfer times
- Significant correlation between mean reaction time and total corpus callosum size
- Callosal size relates to information processing speed, not auditory lateralization
- Individual differences in callosal anatomy do not predict bilateral auditory processing differences

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Important constraint: individual anatomical differences in the corpus callosum do not predict how people lateralize auditory input. This means Aureonoise's bilateral stimulation parameters do not need to be calibrated to individual callosal anatomy. The processing speed correlation suggests that the temporal precision of bilateral alternation (onset timing) matters more than the spatial pathway.
