# 040 — taVNS Effects on HRV

## Metadata
- **Title:** The Acute Effects of Varying Frequency and Pulse Width of Transcutaneous Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation on Heart Rate Variability in Healthy Adults: A Randomized Crossover Controlled Trial
- **Authors:** Peter Atanackov, Jakob Peterlin, Maja Derlink, Uros Kovacic, Natasa Kejzar, Fajko F Bajrovic
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Biomedicines
- **DOI:** 10.3390/biomedicines13030700
- **PMC:** PMC11940630
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11940630/

## Abstract
Heart rate variability reflects autonomic regulation and predicts health outcomes. Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation shows therapeutic promise, yet optimal parameters remain unclear. This randomized crossover trial involved 78 healthy adults across seven sessions testing six active taVNS protocols or sham conditions at the cymba conchae. Protocols varied by frequency (10 Hz or 25 Hz) and pulse width (100-500 us). Sessions included 15-min baseline, 15-min stimulation/sham, and 10-min recovery with continuous ECG recording. Three protocols significantly increased SDNN compared to sham: 10 Hz/250 us, 10 Hz/500 us, and 25 Hz/100 us. No significant RMSSD changes emerged.

## Key Findings
- Specific frequency/pulse width combinations enhance overall HRV (SDNN) acutely
- Inverted U-shaped relationship suggests optimal stimulation parameters exist
- No significant effects on vagally mediated HRV (RMSSD)
- taVNS demonstrated safety with minimal adverse effects
- 10 Hz stimulation more consistently effective than 25 Hz

## Relevance to Aureonoise
Vagal stimulation at 10 Hz (alpha band) enhances HRV. While Aureonoise uses audio rather than electrical stimulation, the vagal pathway is relevant because auditory stimulation can modulate vagal tone through the auricular branch of the vagus nerve (which innervates the ear canal). The 10 Hz optimal frequency matches Aureonoise's alpha range. The inverted-U dose-response parallels the stochastic resonance findings (papers 030-032).
